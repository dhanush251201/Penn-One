      How the optimizer works. Every 30 seconds each waiting request goes through the five steps above. With the re-optimizing engine, whenever new requests arrive the dispatcher then re-plans every rider still waiting for pickup across the whole fleet: riders on board keep their van, a rider is locked once their van is 3 minutes away, nobody changes van more than once, and every quoted pickup still holds. Those changes appear in the log as re-planned. All distances follow physical routes. Walking is the shortest path over OpenStreetMap sidewalks, footpaths, crosswalks and steps, so a 2-minute limit (about 156 m at 1.3 m/s) never cuts through a building, the rail yards or the river. Van screening and insertion use OSRM drive times over the real road network, one-way streets included. Walk-to points are ranked by how many pickups happened near them over 30 past evenings. The screen keeps the 4 vans with the shortest road ETA for the full insertion search.

      Demand is synthetic: 300 requests on one generated evening, with history built from 30 more. Fleet: 5 electric vans with 6 seats and 1 gas van with 8 seats. Drive times come from OSRM, map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, and building positions are approximate. Vans, buses and walking riders are drawn along their actual street and sidewalk routes.
